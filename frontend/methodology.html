Quantitative Methodology & Algorithmic Specs

Scientific documentation of cumulative orderbook depth, liquidation cascades, and imbalance formulas.

1. Stop Loss Cluster Zone ($P_{stop}$) Definition

In cryptocurrency retail markets, stop loss orders are systematically clustered just beneath key 24-hour swing lows (for long positions) and above key swing highs (for short positions).

The algorithm defines the cluster thresholds as follows:

$$P_{stop\_long} = \text{SwingLow}_{24h} \times (1 - 0.005)$$ $$P_{stop\_short} = \text{SwingHigh}_{24h} \times (1 + 0.005)$$

A -0.5% margin below the low (or +0.5% above the high) is applied to capture the exact cluster zone where cascade liquidations trigger.

2. Cumulative Depth Trigger Cost (USDT)

Rather than relying on superficial top-of-book quotes, the terminal computes the exact cumulative net capital required to consume all resting limit orders up to the cluster zone.

$$\text{Trigger Cost (Long Cascade)} = \sum_{P_{bid} \ge P_{stop\_long}} (P_{bid} \times Q_{bid})$$ $$\text{Trigger Cost (Short Squeeze)} = \sum_{P_{ask} \le P_{stop\_short}} (P_{ask} \times Q_{ask})$$

This metric reveals the true market depth: exactly how many USDT of aggressive market selling (or buying) would blow through support (or resistance) and initiate a liquidation domino effect.

3. Orderbook Imbalance Index ($\pm 3\%$)

Evaluates the supply-demand asymmetry within a tight $\pm 3\%$ price corridor from the mid-market price:

$$\text{Bid Ratio} = \frac{\sum_{P_{bid} \ge 0.97 \times P} (P_{bid} \times Q_{bid})}{\text{Total Volume}_{\pm 3\%}} \times 100\%$$

When the Bid Ratio drops below 20%, the asset exhibits acute structural sell-side vulnerability.

4. Dual-Score Algorithmic Evaluation

  • 🎯 Breakout Probability (0 - 99): Quantifies the likelihood of hitting the cluster line. Higher scores indicate tighter distance to the barrier (< 3%), low cumulative trigger cost (< $10,000), and severe orderbook imbalance.
  • 💣 Impact Multiplier (0 - 99): Quantifies the post-break volatility potential (liquidity trap severity). It compares 24h trading volume against the fragile orderbook wall. An asset trading $500k/day backed by only $3k of orderbook depth will experience a violent reaction upon breach.
